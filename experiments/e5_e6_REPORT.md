# E5 and E6: a larger chemistry set, spin states, and workflow routing (exploratory, not pre-registered)

Neither experiment is in PLAN.md. Both were designed after E1 to E4 and should be reported as exploratory.
All numbers below are copied from `experiments/e5_results.json` and `experiments/e6_results.json`. The figure is
`paper/figs/fig5_scaleup.{pdf,png}`, generated by `experiments/e6_figure.py`.

Jev usage: 372 (E5 full) + 372 (E5 geometry-only) + 96 (spin) + 348 (E6) = **1,188 new calls**, all cached in `data/cache`. At most 6 threads were used.

## Labelling protocol (v2, coordinator instruction after peer review of the original labels)
E5 closed-shell labels come from `experiments/qc_labels_v2.label(geometry, 'cc-pvdz')`:
- internally stable RHF;
- the lowest of several stability-followed UHF starts; `rhf_unstable` means a UHF drop > 1 mEh (primary), with 0.1 mEh kept as a sensitivity check;
- full-valence singlet CASCI on RHF orbitals, `mr` = c_HF^2 < 0.9;
- frozen-core CCSD T1 > 0.02, with no label when CCSD did not converge.

Exclusions:
- per label, all cases whose RHF did not converge;
- for `mr`, also cases whose CASCI root is not a singlet (<S^2> > 0.1 despite `fix_spin_`);
- for `unstable`, also cases where no UHF start converged;
- for `t1`, also cases where CCSD did not converge.

No case had `cas_split_degenerate` = True, so the `mr` and `mr_incl_split` rows are identical.

E6 uses `qc_labels_v2.stable_rhf` as the CCSD(T) reference.

The Jev prompts depend only on name, geometry and stretch, so they did not change when the labels were recomputed.

v1 and v2 labels agree on 93.5% of cases for mr, 88.7% for T1 (cases converged in both) and 95.2% for unstable at 0.1 mEh.

- **Ranking.** Jev's AUROC does not differ significantly from the logistic baseline for any label. For T1 the CI touches zero.
- **Calibration.** Jev's Brier score is never better than the base rate (0.203 vs 0.231 for mr; 0.193 vs 0.215 for T1; 0.206 vs 0.206 for unstable). It is always worse than the logistic baseline.
- **Compressed probabilities.** Jev's mean p(mr) rises from 0.42 at f=1.0 to 0.58 at f=2.5, while the true mr rate rises from 0.10 to 0.91. T1 and unstable show the same compression: mean p 0.46 → 0.67 and 0.47 → 0.59.

## E5 spin-state task (32 species)
**Species and experimental or accepted ground-state multiplicity.** These are recorded as a sanity check only; they are not the label.
- Singlet ground state: Be, BH, AlH, C2, CN⁻, BeO, CO, SiH2, CF2, CCl2, PH2⁺, NH2⁻, SiF2, CHF, CHCl.
- Triplet ground state: C, O, Si, NH, O2, NF, S2, SO, PH, PF, NCl, B2, SiC, BN (X³Π; a¹Σ⁺ very close), NO⁻, CH2, NH2⁺.

**Ground truth.** Frozen-core CCSD(T)/cc-pVDZ, adiabatic.
- **Geometry.** Each multiplicity is optimised separately on the CCSD(T) energy itself, within a fixed point-group parameterisation (diatomic r; C2v r and angle; Cs HCX r_CH, r_CX and angle), using bounded scalar or Nelder-Mead search.
  - **Deviation from the brief**, which asked for a fixed geometry. A single fixed geometry biases carbene-type gaps. As a check, the vertical ordering at the ground-state geometry agrees with the adiabatic ordering for all 32 species.
- **Singlet.** RHF → RCCSD(T).
- **Triplet and quintet.** UHF → UCCSD(T). <S²> of the UHF reference is 2.00 to 2.08 for triplets, except CO (2.27), and 6.00 to 6.07 for quintets. ROHF-UCCSD(T) at the same geometries is stored under `rohf_check`.
- **Reference choice.**
  - A first version that used a single SCF solution gave C2 a singlet about 60 mEh too high and a spurious (T) collapse for BN. That run was discarded and the script rewritten.
  - The final version builds several SCF solutions (3 guesses, with and without stability following, plus occupation swaps for open shells). It keeps the lowest *sane* CCSD(T): converged, T1 < 0.08, and |E(T)| < 15% of E_corr.
- **Quintet.** Optimised only where it lies within 0.10 Eh vertically: B2 (quintet 7.2 mEh above the triplet) and SiC. Elsewhere its vertical value is used. For Be the quintet reference is unphysical (4.3 Eh above the singlet), which is irrelevant to the label.
- **Post-hoc fix.** For C2 the optimised triplet (+64.5 mEh) was higher than the triplet at the singlet geometry (+8.5 mEh), so the search had stalled on a worse SCF branch. The lower value is used, and the fix is recorded in `optimisation_fixups`.
- **BN.** No sane CCSD(T) was found for its lowest (singlet) state.
- **Agreement with experiment.** CCSD(T) agrees with the experimental assignment for 31 of 32 species. The exception is BN: CCSD(T) says singlet, 8.3 mEh below the triplet, while experiment says X³Π.
- **Near-degenerate species** (gap to the next multiplicity ≤ 10 mEh): C2 8.5, B2 7.2, SiC 6.2, BN 8.3, CHCl 6.9 mEh. None falls below 5 mEh.
- **Classes.** 16 singlets and 16 triplets; no species has a quintet ground state.

**Jev input.** state = {system "<formula> (atom / molecule / molecular anion / molecular cation)", charge, electron_count, method_context "electronic structure; compare the lowest singlet, triplet and quintet states, each at its own optimized geometry"}. Question: Choice "Which spin multiplicity does the ground (lowest-energy) electronic state of this species have?" over {1 singlet, 3 triplet, 5 quintet}. R=3; the prediction is the argmax of the mean probabilities.

| set | n | Jev accuracy | Jev Brier (3-class) | Jev log loss | always-singlet acc. / Brier | class-frequency acc. / Brier | HF-energy ordering acc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all (CCSD(T) labels) | 32 | 0.81 | 0.277 | 0.467 | 0.50 / 1.000 | 0.50 / 0.500 | 0.78 |
| gap > 10 mEh | 27 | 0.85 | 0.251 | 0.432 | 0.48 / 1.037 | 0.52 / 0.499 | 0.89 |
| all (experimental labels) | 32 | 0.84 | 0.272 | 0.461 | 0.47 / 1.063 | 0.53 / 0.498 | 0.81 |

- **Errors against CCSD(T).** Jev gets BH, SiC, BN, CF2, CCl2 and PH2⁺ wrong. BN is an error only against CCSD(T); against experiment it is correct.
- **The errors are singlets called triplet.** Every species with a triplet ground state gets argmax triplet from Jev except SiC (p(3)=0.35). For singlet ground states, Jev puts p(triplet) between 0.02 and 0.50. It hedges near 0.5 on the heavier or less familiar singlets (BH 0.47, SiH2 0.47, CF2 0.48, CCl2 0.50, PH2⁺ 0.49).
- **Quintet.** Jev never puts more than 0.23 on the quintet (PF).
- **HF baseline.** The "HF-energy ordering" baseline uses the HF energies of the selected references at the CCSD(T) geometries. It is therefore not a free baseline, but it shows that a single cheap computation matches Jev.
- **No obvious textbook rule** beyond "always singlet" was found, so none is reported.

## E6 workflow routing
**Cases.** 116 in total:
- 12 E3 molecules (qc_labels geometries) in STO-3G;
- 12 E5 molecules in STO-3G: HCN, C2H2, CH2O, N2H2, O3, LiF, MgO, Be2, SiO, P2, BF, HCl;
- H2, LiH, BH, HF and H2O in 6-31G;
- each × {1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5}.

**Method.** All electrons are correlated in both CCSD(T) and FCI. The reference is `stable_rhf`.

**FCI.** Two Davidson runs:
- A: spin penalty 0.2, 3 roots;
- B: no penalty, 4 roots.

E_FCI is the lowest root with <S²> < 0.01. A case is accepted only if run A converged to a singlet, run B found either no singlet or the same energy (within 1e-5 Eh), and E_FCI ≤ E_RHF.

A first FCI pass (one `fix_spin_` run with default settings) returned unconverged or non-singlet roots for several stretched cases; for example, N2H2 ×2.0 came out 1.4 Eh too high. It was discarded and every case was recomputed.

**Label.** CCSD(T) is reliable when CCSD converged and |E_CCSD(T) − E_FCI| < 1.6 mEh.

**Excluded (19; 97 used):**
- CCSD not converged: N2×1.5/2.0/2.5, C2×2.0, H2O/STO-3G×2.5, CH2O×2.0, N2H2×2.5, H2O/6-31G×2.5.
- RHF not converged: HCN×2.5; also CH2O×2.5, HCl×2.5 (both also CCSD not converged).
- FCI check failed: C2×2.5, C2H2×2.5, N2H2×2.0 (all three also CCSD not converged); MgO×1.5 and MgO×2.0 (the runs disagree).
- RHF, CCSD and FCI all failed: O3×1.5/2.0/2.5.

**Jev input.** `state_of` with "cc-pVDZ" replaced by "STO-3G" or "6-31G"; statement `S['ccsdt_ok']` verbatim, asked alone, R=3.

**Error distribution.** CCSD(T) fails in 37 of 97 cases (38%): 35 of 78 in STO-3G and 2 of 19 in 6-31G. By stretch: f=1.0 8/29, 1.5 12/26, 2.0 11/23, 2.5 6/19. |error| median 0.62 mEh, mean 23.4 mEh, max 194 mEh.
- Several equilibrium STO-3G failures are small but above threshold: N2 2.2, C2H2 1.9, HCN 2.1, O3 3.9, P2 3.6, MgO 1.75 mEh.

**Discrimination of failures (AUROC).** Jev's AUROC is 0.76 [0.65, 0.85]. The CI for Jev − T1 is [−0.03, 0.24]. Of the alternatives:

| predictor | AUROC for failure |
|---|---|
| Jev (1 − p) | 0.76 [0.65, 0.85] |
| T1 | 0.65 |
| LOO logistic (stretch, gap) | 0.50 |
| stretch alone | 0.54 |

**Calibration.** Jev is badly miscalibrated for this statement:
- Mean p(ok) is 0.24, against a true reliable rate of 0.62.
- All p values lie between 0.15 and 0.46.
- Brier is 0.358, against 0.236 for the base rate.

**Routing policies.** The expensive MR method is assumed exact. Each policy's result is the fraction of cases whose final |error| exceeds 1.6 mEh.

- **Area under the routing curve** (fraction routed vs failure fraction; lower is better): Jev 0.130, logistic 0.190, random routing 0.191.
- **Routing needed to hold the failure fraction down.** Jev's ranking needs 54% routed to reach ≤ 10% failures and 65% to reach ≤ 5%. The logistic needs 64% and 79%.
- **Jev's natural threshold** (p < 0.5) routes everything, because every p is below 0.46. Jev is useful here only as a ranking whose threshold must be set by the user.

## Caveats
1. E5 and E6 are exploratory and were not pre-registered.
2. E5 geometries are recalled experimental values, with H2O2 and HOF marked approximate. Stretched geometries scale every coordinate, which is not a chemically meaningful dissociation path for polyatomics.
3. E5 labels depend on the RHF solution. Many stretched cases have c_HF² ≈ 0 because the stable RHF at large stretch is a qualitatively different determinant. Labels are recomputed with v2; the v1 labels (`e5_labels.json`) differ on 5% to 11% of cases per label.
4. Spin ground truth:
   - Single-reference CCSD(T) is used for C2, BN, B2 and SiC, which are multireference.
   - Open-shell singlets (O2 ¹Δ, NH ¹Δ and similar) are described by a closed-shell RHF reference only. This biases those singlets upward, in the direction of the true (triplet) answer.
   - The "sane reference" rule is a researcher choice.
   - The C2 triplet energy needed the vertical fix-up above.
   - CCSD(T) disagrees with experiment for BN.
5. Spin classes are balanced 16/16 by construction of the list. Accuracy therefore depends on which species were chosen, and the always-singlet baseline scores 0.50 by design.
6. E6 is mostly STO-3G (78 of 97 cases). "Reliable" is set by a 1.6 mEh threshold, which small-basis artefacts cross even at equilibrium. The 19 excluded cases are the hard ones (non-convergence), and the sensitivity analysis above covers them.
7. In the routing simulation, Jev and logistic curves sweep thresholds in-sample. Jev's threshold is not tuned, so its useful operating point is not identified by its own probabilities.
