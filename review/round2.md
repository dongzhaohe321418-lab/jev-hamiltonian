## New issues

### Blocking

None. Every issue below can be fixed in the text or in the analysis of cached data, and none needs new API calls.

### Major

- **N1. The stretch-baseline mislabel (mismatches 1–2) undercuts the E3/E5 headline comparison.** The paper's own thesis is that the skill "mostly reflects a stretch factor stated in the prompt". The bare stretch factor beats Jev on multireference (0.92 vs 0.76) and instability (0.93 vs 0.89) by more than the table shows, and on the v2 labels Jev's T1 lead over the bare stretch (0.81 vs 0.78) is smaller than the table implies (0.81 vs 0.71). Readers of Table 1 will conclude that Jev beats "the stretch factor" on T1 by 0.10 AUROC. Round 1 specifically asked for the unfitted baseline (R3-M1).
- **N2. The E6 claim "A rule that routes every stretched geometry did better than both" (line 244) and the Discussion's "a plain rule that routes stretched geometries was better still" (line 311) are contradicted by the routing curve.** The stretch rule routes 68 of 97 cases and leaves 8 failures. At the same budget of 68 routed, Jev's ranking leaves **3** failures, and at 64–66 routed it leaves 3 (reviewer check on `e6_results.json` `curves.jev`). Fig. 5c shows this: the stretch marker lies above the Jev curve. The stretch rule beats the T1 rule and Jev *at the T1 budget* only because it routes 23 percentage points more. Compare at matched budget, or say that the stretch rule buys its lower failure count by routing more. The Jev curve sweeps thresholds in-sample (E5/E6 REPORT caveat 7), and that should be stated too.
- **N3. The E6 exclusions are not disclosed.** 116 cases were run and 19 excluded, 16 of them for CCSD, RHF or FCI non-convergence (`e5_e6_REPORT.md`, "Excluded"). Those are the cases most likely to be CCSD(T) failures. The paper says only "For 97 small cases where full CI is affordable". The sensitivity analysis that counts the 8 CCSD-non-converged cases as failures exists and supports the result (Jev AUROC 0.78 vs T1 0.64). One sentence reporting the exclusions and this sensitivity would close the gap. The paper should also say that E6 correlates all electrons, unlike E3's frozen core.
- **N4. The E5 statement that Jev "separates multireference from single-reference cases within a single stretch level (AUROC 0.76–0.90) ... so on less familiar molecules it draws on more than the stated stretch" (line 234, repeated in the Discussion, line 310) overreaches.** The strata have 3 positives (f = 1.0) and 7, 3 and 2 negatives (f = 1.5, 2.0, 2.5) (reviewer check on `e5_labels_v2.json`). The 0.90 comes from a stratum with 2 negatives, and no interval is given. The E5/E6 REPORT itself warns that these AUROCs "are noisy" and "should not be interpreted". Within-stratum signal can also come from the molecule name rather than from "less familiar" chemistry. Report CIs, or a pooled stratified test (for example a logistic model on stretch with and without logit p_Jev, LOMO, compared by log loss). Otherwise downgrade this to "weak evidence".
- **N5. The held-out Ising test does not discriminate a joint landscape from an asymmetric dependency network, yet the abstract's lead claim rests on it.** Fitting a symmetric pairwise Ising model to single-fact conditionals and predicting two-fact conditionals rewards additivity of fact effects in logit space. An unconstrained, asymmetric additive-logit model (a dependency network with J_ab ≠ J_ba) would also beat "ignore the second fact", and probably fit better. The paper's own evidence (residual 5× noise, 41% significant asymmetries) says the symmetric constraint does not hold. The phrase "contain an approximate pairwise landscape" needs one more comparison: symmetric Ising vs asymmetric additive model on the same held-out two-fact conditionals. If the asymmetric model is clearly better, the abstract should say that facts combine approximately additively, not that one landscape exists.
- **N6. The pre-registered primary criterion is silently absent.** This belongs with R1-M3/R3-M3 but is new in the revision. PLAN.md deviation 2 says the corrected value is reported and the criterion is described as descriptive. The paper contains neither. Report the corrected 3×-noise-floor fraction as the pre-registered primary result, labelled descriptive (Codex's recomputation was 53%), or state in the paper that it was dropped and why.

## Verdict

**Major revision, with no blocking issues and no new data needed.** The revision takes round 1 seriously. The labels are rebuilt, the null is matched per pair with FDR control, clustered CIs and LOMO baselines are in place, the positive control and ablations have been run, and E4 is rebuilt with fair comparators. The new E5–E7 material widens the evidence. Most round-1 major concerns are resolved and the rest are partially resolved.

Before this goes to arXiv, the authors should:

1. Relabel or replace the "stretch factor" baseline everywhere, add the unfitted 0.92/0.78/0.93 (and 0.87/0.69/0.86 for E5), and make line 207 internally consistent (N1, mismatches 1–2).
2. Correct the E6 routing claims: compare at matched budget, fix the mean-error description and disclose the 19 exclusions (N2, N3, mismatch 3).
3. Temper the E5 within-stretch claim, or support it with intervals or a pooled test (N4).
4. Add the asymmetric-additive comparison to the held-out Ising test, or soften "approximate pairwise landscape" (N5).
5. Report or explicitly drop the pre-registered E1 criterion, and bring the paper's deviation list into line with PLAN.md (N6).
6. Fix mismatches 4–8 and the minors.
