# Referee report R1 (pre-submission, arXiv cs.LG)

Manuscript: "Is there a Hamiltonian inside a decision model? Probing the energy landscape of Jev for quantum chemistry"

Emphasis brief: statistical and machine-learning soundness.

Assessment boundary: I read `paper/main.tex`, the compiled figures, `experiments/PLAN.md` and its git history, the harness, all experiment and analysis scripts, and the cached results. I re-ran `experiments/analyze_e1e3.py` and `experiments/analyze_e4.py` offline (outputs reproduced the manuscript numbers bit for bit and left the tracked results files unchanged) and ran several offline re-analyses of the released JSON files in a scratch directory. No API call was made and no project file other than this report was modified. Where I quote a number that is not in the manuscript, it comes from these offline re-analyses of the released data and is labelled as a reviewer check.

## Overall assessment

The manuscript asks a crisp question: if a model returns calibrated distributions for many typed questions about one input, do the implied local energies assemble into one joint landscape, and is that landscape usable in quantum chemistry? It answers with four black-box probes (E1 conditional compatibility, E2 in-context Hamiltonians, E3 chemistry decisions against PySCF labels, E4 determinant ranking for selected CI). The engineering is clean: every response is cached, the analysis scripts are short and deterministic, and the headline numbers I checked are reproduced exactly from the cache.

The statistical case is weaker than the prose suggests, in four places that bear on the abstract. First, the claim that the logically forced coupling (multireference versus RHF adequate) is symmetric holds only after averaging over cases; at the case level that pair is among the most asymmetric in the data. Second, E1 tests a composite hypothesis (a joint exists and textual "established fact" statements act as probabilistic conditioning) without the positive control that the pre-registered plan promised, so the step from asymmetry to "no exact joint" is not identified. Third, the E3 ranking skill is almost entirely explained by the stretch factor printed in the prompt; within a stretch level Jev's discrimination is near chance, and the geometry-only ablation removes both the name and the stretch note, so the abstract's attribution to the molecule name is not supported. Fourth, the pre-registration statement overstates what was pre-specified: the E1 statement set changed, a pre-registered control and pre-registered metrics are missing, and several post hoc analyses are not labelled exploratory.

None of these problems is fatal to a useful short paper. Most are resolved by re-analysis that the released data already permit, plus claim moderation. In its present form, however, the abstract claims more than the numbers establish.

## Who would be interested in the results, and why

Researchers evaluating the logical coherence of probabilistic model outputs (consistency checks on forecasters and language models) would find the pairwise compatibility protocol a natural extension from identity checks to joint-distribution checks. Practitioners considering cheap ML triage before electronic-structure calculations would be interested in the E3 and E4 negative and mixed results. The quantum-computing and QUBO community may find E2 a cautionary example about models reading Hamiltonians from text. The audience is interdisciplinary but narrow, because the object of study is a single proprietary model version, and the generalisable contribution is the protocol rather than the Jev-specific findings.

## Major strengths

1. Full provenance. Every one of the 2,235 requests is cached with request body, repeat index, latency and timestamp; all responses report `jev-1.13.0`; the E1/E3 and E4 analyses reproduce the manuscript numbers exactly from the cache.
2. Repeats are built into the design (three per query, with a pilot establishing that outputs are stochastic), and noise is at least considered when interpreting E1.
3. The E2b convention-flip control is a well-chosen intervention: identical energies with opposite printed signs reverse the rank correlation at every n (reviewer check at the instance level: 0 or 1 of 10 instances positive in E2 for n = 4 to 7, 8 to 10 of 10 positive in E2b for n = 4 to 7). This is the most convincing single result in the paper.
4. The E3 comparison includes sensible cheap baselines (base rate, one- and two-feature logistic regression), and the E4 comparison includes a strong free baseline (first-order perturbative estimate) that the manuscript honestly reports Jev losing to.
5. The paper reports negative results plainly and includes a limitations paragraph that names the single-model, small-sample and prompt-wording constraints.

## Major Concerns

R1-M1 [statistical-rigor]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Axis** statistical-rigor
**Claim pointer** The abstract and Section E1 state that the logically forced coupling between "multireference" and "RHF qualitatively correct" is symmetric (J = -2.43 and -2.35), and the Discussion repeats that "the strongest logical coupling is symmetric".
**Evidence pointer** Abstract; Results E1, second paragraph; Discussion, first paragraph; `experiments/e1e3_results.json` (`J_pairs[0]`, `asym`, `se`); `analyze_e1e3.py` lines printing `J(mr|rhf_ok)`.
**Concern** The two quoted values are means over 48 cases of J(mr given rhf_ok) and J(rhf_ok given mr). The symmetry test in the paper is defined per case. Per case, this pair is among the least symmetric of the ten. Reviewer check on the released `asym` and `se` arrays: median |J_ab - J_ba| for this pair is 0.55 nats (overall median 0.26), 77% of its 48 cases exceed three bootstrap SE (overall 57%), and 65% exceed the paper's own coherent-null 95th percentile (overall 35%). Only the rhf_ok/unstable pair is worse (median 0.54, 90% beyond 3 SE). Case-level values in `J_pairs[0]` swing in both directions (for example -2.87 against -2.04 and -1.79 against -2.63), and the errors cancel in the mean. The pair is presented as evidence that the conditioning mechanism works where logic forces the answer. The data show the opposite.
**Why it matters** This is the only in-data check that textual conditioning behaves as probabilistic conditioning, and it is used in the abstract to frame the result as "close to, but not exactly" coherent. Reported at the level the test is defined, it removes the reassurance and changes how the rest of E1 should be read.
**Resolution test** Report the per-pair distribution of case-level asymmetry (for example a per-pair version of Fig. 2b or a table of median asymmetry and the fraction beyond the null for each of the ten pairs). Remove or correct "symmetric" in the abstract, Results and Discussion. If a mean-level statement is kept, say explicitly that it averages over cases and that the case-level asymmetry is large.

R1-M2 [mechanism-evidence]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Axis** mechanism-evidence (construct validity of the compatibility test)
**Claim pointer** E1 concludes that Jev's outputs "behave like a dependency network: a set of approximately compatible local energies with no exact joint behind them", and contribution (1) offers the protocol as a black-box test of whether a model has one landscape.
**Evidence pointer** Results E1; Introduction contributions; `experiments/e1e3_collect.py` (`FACT`, `state_of`, `jobs`); `experiments/PLAN.md` (E1 "Positive control"); `experiments/e0_basics.py`.
**Concern** Coupling symmetry is a property of the model's conditionals p(a given b). What the experiment measures is p(a given the text "Established fact (computed): b ... This statement is TRUE/FALSE"). An asymmetry can therefore arise because no joint exists, or because a joint exists but the model does not treat an asserted fact as an observation of b (for example it discounts asserted facts of one type more than another, or reads "FALSE" differently from "TRUE"). The test is of the conjunction, and the manuscript attributes any failure to the first conjunct. Two further design features add alternative explanations. The marginal query asks all five statements while each conditional query drops the conditioned statement and adds a `known` field, so the question set and state schema differ between the terms of the total-probability identity. The cross-talk pilot that licenses treating these as the same context used one molecule, one "alone" call per question, and no `known` field. The pre-registered plan anticipated this and specified a positive control ("statements with a known logical/arithmetical coupling ... applied to an exact Ising model's conditionals, and to a set of number statements"). No such control appears in the code or the manuscript; the "coherent null" is a numerical simulation of noise and rounding, not a probe of Jev.
**Why it matters** Without a positive control, the protocol offered as the paper's first contribution has not been shown to detect coherence when coherence is present, so neither "no exact joint" nor the dependency-network interpretation is identified. R1-M1 removes the only in-data support.
**Resolution test** Run the pre-registered positive control. One option is to state a fully specified small joint in the input (for example a 2x2 table, or two statements with an arithmetic relation) and check whether Jev's textual conditionals recover a symmetric J. A second is to query conditioning with the conditioned statement retained as a question, so the marginal and conditional contexts match. A third is a cross-talk check that includes the `known` field on several molecules. If the control cannot be run, restate the E1 conclusion as "Jev's responses to asserted facts are not compatible with any joint", and drop "no exact joint behind them".

R1-M3 [reproducibility]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Axis** reproducibility (honesty of pre-registration claims)
**Claim pointer** Setup states that the analyses of E1 to E4 were written down before the main data were collected, that checks added after seeing data are labelled exploratory, and that the plan and its amendments are in the repository.
**Evidence pointer** Setup, "Pre-registration"; `experiments/PLAN.md` at commit 8ee0321 and its amendment at ed07a14; cache timestamps; `analyze_e1e3.py`; `analyze_e4.py`; `e4_selected_ci.py`.
**Concern** The timing is consistent with the claim: the pilot cache entries are stamped 22:28, the plan was committed at 22:29:07, and all other entries are stamped 22:30 or later (local git history, not an external registry). The content does not match the claim, in several respects.
(a) The pre-registered E1 statements included "open-shell ground state" and "low-lying excited state within 1 eV". The study used "T1 > 0.02" and "RHF unstable" instead. This change appears neither in the amendments nor in the manuscript.
(b) The pre-registered E1 positive control was not run (see R1-M2).
(c) The plan says every probe "reports means with bootstrap 95% CIs". The manuscript reports no confidence interval for any quantity.
(d) The pre-registered E2 metric "maximum-likelihood inverse temperature beta" is not reported.
(e) Several analyses that are not in the plan are presented without an exploratory label: the coherent-null simulation, the field and coupling decomposition in E2, the diagonal-energy baseline in E4 (present in the first E4 commit but not in the plan or the amendments), and the E4 summary statistic (mean log10 error over the whole curve) with its four Wilcoxon tests.
(f) The E1 primary criterion was "3x the noise floor (estimated from repeats)", which the paper operationalises as three bootstrap SE. This is a defensible reading but should be stated as one.
(g) The plan specified about 60 E3 cases; 48 were used.
**Why it matters** Pre-registration is offered as a guard against analytic flexibility, and readers will weight the E1 and E4 results by it. An undisclosed change of variables in the primary experiment, together with unlabelled post hoc analyses, makes the statement in Setup inaccurate as written.
**Resolution test** Add a deviations table (plan item, what was done, reason, whether decided before or after seeing data) covering items (a) to (g). Label the post hoc analyses exploratory in the text. Either report the pre-registered CIs and beta, or state that they were dropped and why.

R1-M4 [experimental-design]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Axis** experimental-design (baseline and ablation logic in E3)
**Claim pointer** The abstract states that Jev ranks multireference character, T1 and RHF instability "comparable to a two-feature logistic model" and "falls to 0.60 to 0.68 when the molecule's name is removed". Results E3 concludes that most of the skill comes from recognising a named molecule and a stated stretch. The Discussion and abstract recommend Jev as a "fast triage layer".
**Evidence pointer** Abstract; Results E3 and Table 1; `experiments/qc_labels.json`; `experiments/e1e3_collect.py` (`geometry_note`); `experiments/e1b_e3b.py` (`geo_job`).
**Concern** Every Jev prompt contains "all bonds stretched to f x equilibrium length", and the labels are close to a deterministic function of f. At f = 1.0 the positives are 1, 2 and 3 of 12 (MR, T1, unstable), and at f = 2.0 they are 10, 12 and 12 of 12. A one-feature logistic model on f already reaches AUROC 0.79, 0.76 and 0.87. The informative question is whether Jev discriminates within a stretch level. Reviewer check on `Pm` and `labels` in the released results: within f = 1.5, the only stratum with balanced labels, Jev's AUROC is 0.67 (MR, 6 of 12 positive), 0.54 (T1, 6 of 12) and 0.48 (unstable, 10 of 12). Within f = 2.0 for MR it is 0.17 (10 of 12). These strata are tiny, but none of them shows skill beyond the printed stretch factor. The geometry-only ablation removes both the name and the stretch note, so it cannot attribute the loss to the name, and the abstract's "when the molecule's name is removed" misdescribes it.
**Why it matters** If Jev's ranking reduces to reading back a number the user wrote into the prompt, the recommendation to use it as a triage layer before any calculation is unsupported: the same user already has that information for free. This recommendation is one of the paper's two practical conclusions.
**Resolution test** Report stratified or conditional discrimination: AUROC within stretch level, or a logistic model of the label on f plus logit(p_Jev) with a likelihood-ratio test for the Jev term, using molecule-clustered inference. Run the two single-cue ablations separately (name removed with stretch note kept, and stretch note removed with name kept) on the cached-prompt template. Correct the abstract's description of the ablation. Moderate the triage recommendation unless Jev adds information beyond f.

R1-M5 [statistical-rigor]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** statistical-rigor (noise and null calibration in E1)
**Claim pointer** 57% of pair-case asymmetries exceed three bootstrap SE (the pre-registered criterion); 35% exceed the 95th percentile of a coherent null; total-probability violations are "well above the per-call noise".
**Evidence pointer** Results E1; Fig. 2b and 2c; `analyze_e1e3.py` (bootstrap loop, `noise_sd`, coherent-null loop).
**Concern** The two headline fractions differ by a factor of 1.6, and neither is calibrated against its own false-positive rate.
(i) With three repeats, a bootstrap offers at most 10 distinct resamples per cell and underestimates the SE, so "three bootstrap SE" does not carry its Gaussian meaning. Reviewer check: applying the paper's exact criterion to data simulated from a coherent joint matched case by case to Jev's own conditionals gives about 4.5% exceedances (at noise s.d. 0.013 or 0.020), about 15 times the nominal Gaussian rate. This should be reported alongside the 57%.
(ii) `noise_sd` is the mean of per-cell standard deviations computed with ddof = 0 from three draws, which is biased low. The unbiased mean is 0.016 and the root mean square is 0.020 (reviewer check), against the reported 0.013. The noise is also heteroscedastic: the mean s.d. is about 0.018 for p in [0.2, 0.8] and about 0.009 outside it.
(iii) The coherent null draws fields and couplings from N(0,1) and N(0,1.5), which are not matched to Jev's probability range, and pools every pair into one percentile band.
(iv) Fig. 2c compares |residual| of three-call averages with the per-call s.d., which is not the null distribution of the residual.
(v) The 480 pair-case tests are treated as exchangeable although they share marginals within a case and molecules across stretches. No interval is given for either fraction.
In the other direction, a reviewer check with a per-pair null matched to Jev's conditionals and s.d. 0.020 puts about 50% of pairs beyond their own 95th percentile. The qualitative conclusion that asymmetry exceeds noise therefore appears robust, but the numbers in the abstract are not interpretable as stated.
**Why it matters** The abstract's quantitative characterisation of incoherence ("57% ... beyond three bootstrap standard errors") is the paper's lead number, and readers cannot judge it without the corresponding null rate.
**Resolution test** Replace the pooled null with a per-case matched parametric null using unbiased, heteroscedastic noise estimates. Report the null exceedance rate next to each observed rate, and give molecule-clustered bootstrap intervals for the fractions and medians. Use the same null for the total-probability residuals in Fig. 2c.

R1-M6 [statistical-rigor]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** statistical-rigor (uncertainty, units of analysis, comparisons)
**Claim pointer** Table 1 bolds the best predictor per metric. The text says Jev is "comparable" to the logistic model but worse in Brier score for all labels. Fig. 3 reports mean ± s.e.m. over 10 instances x 3 repeats. E2b states that top-1 accuracy "rose above chance for n ≤ 6".
**Evidence pointer** Table 1; Results E2 and E3; Fig. 3 caption; `figures.py` (`summ`).
**Concern** No confidence interval or test accompanies any E3 comparison, although n = 48 cases from 12 molecules. Reviewer check with a molecule-level cluster bootstrap: Jev's MR AUROC 95% interval is roughly [0.60, 0.91], and the Jev minus two-feature-logistic AUROC differences have intervals spanning zero for all three labels ([-0.23, 0.08], [-0.07, 0.14], [-0.12, 0.11]). The bolding in Table 1 therefore ranks predictors that the data cannot separate. The leave-one-out logistic model leaves out a case while keeping the same molecule at other stretches. A leave-one-molecule-out refit gives similar AUROCs (0.83, 0.82 and 0.90, reviewer check), so the baseline survives, but the manuscript should use and state the clustered scheme. In Fig. 3 the s.e.m. treats the three repeats of an instance as independent (pseudoreplication); the unit is the instance (n = 10). The E2b top-1 claims rest on 30 correlated trials per n with no test; at n = 3 the E2b rank correlation (+0.09) is not distinguishable from zero at the instance level (reviewer check, p about 0.56), so "reversed" does not hold for every n. Across E2 and E3 many comparisons are made with no multiplicity statement (the four E4 Wilcoxon tests do survive a Holm correction).
**Why it matters** Several verbal conclusions ("comparable", "above chance", "reversed") are statistical claims that currently carry no uncertainty, contrary to the pre-registered plan.
**Resolution test** Add molecule-clustered bootstrap CIs (or DeLong intervals with clustering) for AUROC, Brier and ECE, and paired CIs for Jev minus baseline. Compute Fig. 3 error bars over instances. Test the E2 and E2b correlations and top-1 rates at the instance level. Remove bolding that the intervals do not support, and state the multiplicity handling.

R1-M7 [claim-moderation]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** claim-moderation (instrument-limited H2 energy read-out)
**Claim pointer** The abstract says the H2 energy read-out is off by 104 mEh on average, and the Discussion says Jev's energies are "two orders of magnitude from chemical accuracy".
**Evidence pointer** Results E2, last paragraph; Fig. 3c; Discussion, second paragraph; `e2_hamiltonian.py` (`h2_ask`); `experiments/e2_raw.json`.
**Concern** The read-out instrument cannot express chemical accuracy. It is a ten-level Score with 50 mEh spacing, and the exact energies alone have a nearest-level quantisation error of about 7 mEh (reviewer check), already above the 1.6 mEh target. The reported value is the expectation of a nearly flat level distribution, and the modal level in all 24 queries is level 9 (-0.75 Eh, the top of the scale). A constant prediction at the scale midpoint has MAE of about 71 mEh, and the best constant level about 65 mEh (reviewer checks), so Jev is worse than an uninformative constant. That is the informative comparison. The comparison with chemical accuracy is set by the design, not by Jev.
**Why it matters** "Two orders of magnitude from chemical accuracy" reads as a property of the model when it is mostly a property of the scale chosen.
**Resolution test** Report the H2 read-out against constant and quantisation baselines, state the level spacing and the resulting floor, and drop the comparison with chemical accuracy or qualify it as bounded by the instrument. A finer Score or a Choice over narrower bins would be needed to make a chemical-accuracy claim in either direction.

## Minor Comments

R1-m1 [statistical-rigor]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** "for RHF instability the Brier score (0.184) is worse even than that of the constant base rate (0.177)"
**Evidence pointer** Results E3; Table 1; `analyze_e1e3.py` (`base_rate`)
**Issue** The base rate is the in-sample mean of the 48 labels, while the logistic baselines are leave-one-out. A leave-one-out base rate has Brier 0.1842 against Jev's 0.1844 (reviewer check), which is a tie.
**Required correction** Use a leave-one-out (or leave-one-molecule-out) base rate for consistency, and restate the comparison.

R1-m2 [writing-clarity]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** "the standard deviation of a Noul is 0.013 on average (up to about 0.03)"
**Evidence pointer** Setup, "Access and energies"; `analyze_e1e3.py` (`noise_sd`); `PLAN.md` (pilot s.d. "~0.02-0.03")
**Issue** The 0.013 figure is a ddof = 0 mean from three draws. The 95th percentile of the per-cell s.d. is about 0.040, so "up to about 0.03" understates the tail. The sentence also contains a capitalisation error ("noise, In a pilot").
**Required correction** Report an unbiased pooled s.d. and its range or percentiles, and state how many molecules and calls the cross-talk pilot used.

**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Label definitions for E1 and E3

R1-m7 [claim-moderation]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Discussion statements about Gibbs sampling and training
**Evidence pointer** Discussion, first paragraph
**Issue** "A Gibbs sampler run on such conditionals converges to a distribution that depends on the order of updates" is not demonstrated on Jev's conditionals, and "which is what one expects when each head is trained to be calibrated on its own" presumes a training procedure that the paper cannot observe.

R1-m8 [experimental-design]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** E4 interpretation
**Evidence pointer** Results E4, last paragraph; Fig. 4b and 4c; `e4_selected_ci.py` (`curves`)
**Issue** "Part of Jev's disadvantage is resolution" is asserted without a test. The Jev curve is a geometric mean over 30 tie-break orders, which no single deployable ordering achieves, whereas the baselines are single orderings. The summary metric (mean log10 error with a 1e-3 mEh floor, averaged over all k) weights the long tail where all methods converge, and its sensitivity to the floor and to the k range is not shown. Jev is not distinguishable from the excitation-rank heuristic, which the section title "a weak but real prior" does not convey.
**Required correction** Report the metric restricted to the non-tied (nonzero-probability) prefix, show sensitivity to the floor and k range, and retitle or qualify the section to say that Jev's advantage is over random and diagonal ordering only.

R1-m9 [experimental-design]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** E2 metric and E2b interpretation
**Evidence pointer** Results E2; `e2_hamiltonian.py` (`ising_job`)
**Issue** The Choice asks for the minimiser, so probabilities on non-minimal configurations are not required to be ordered by energy even for a model that computes correctly. The Spearman correlation over all 2^n configurations is therefore a proxy whose interpretation should be stated. The E2b flip changes both the printed formula and the coefficient signs, so it cannot separate "reads coefficient signs" from "assumes the textbook meaning of h and J regardless of the stated formula". Both readings imply that the stated functional form is ignored, but the text favours the first. The E2b field correlation at n = 3 is 0.01 (reviewer check), which fits the sign-reading account less well than the other sizes do.
**Required correction** State the proxy assumption, and either add a condition that flips only the formula's sign (coefficients unchanged) or phrase the mechanism as consistent with both readings.

## Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

R1-M1 (case-level asymmetry of the logically forced pair contradicts the "symmetric" claim), R1-M2 (no positive control; textual conditioning not validated as conditioning), R1-M3 (pre-registration deviations undisclosed and post hoc analyses unlabelled), R1-M4 (E3 skill not shown to exceed the stretch factor printed in the prompt; ablation misattributed in the abstract).

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria

- Originality. Applying compatibility conditions for conditional specifications as a black-box test of multi-head decision models is a sensible and, to my reading of the cited literature, uncommon framing. The chemistry probes are straightforward applications.
- Scientific importance. Modest. The findings concern one proprietary model version, and the transferable contribution (the protocol) is not yet validated by a positive control.
- Interdisciplinary readership. Potential readers span ML evaluation, probabilistic coherence and computational chemistry, but the chemistry conclusions are currently limited by the stretch-factor confound and small n.
- Technical soundness. Engineering and provenance are strong, and the E2b intervention is convincing. Inference in E1 and E3 needs re-analysis, uncertainty quantification and a positive control before the abstract's claims hold.
- Readability for nonspecialists. The paper is concise and clearly organised. The energy and Hamiltonian framing is accessible, although E4's selected-CI setup will be opaque to ML readers without a sentence on why determinant ranking matters.

## Recommendation posture

Promising, but the central case is currently not established from the provided evidence. The E2 convention-flip result stands. The E1 and E3 headline claims need re-analysis at the correct unit, a positive control and an honest deviations record before they support the abstract. I expect the work could reach a sound short paper if the blocking items are resolved, largely with the data already cached plus a small number of additional control queries.
