# Adversarial measurement/statistics audit

Scope: `paper/main.tex`, `experiments/PLAN.md`, and the requested E1--E4, figure, and label code.  I ran `analyze_e1e3.py` and `analyze_e4.py` with `.venv/bin/python` using the checked-in cached data; no network request was made.  Line references below are to the source that computes the quantity.

## Findings

1. **`experiments/analyze_e1e3.py:35-38` — the bootstrap resamples a single repeat index for every conditional and every statement within a case, although these are separate API calls.**  `idx` has shape `(case, repeat)` and is broadcast by `take_along_axis` over the condition and variable axes.  Thus, for example, every `p(a|b=1)`, `p(a|b=0)`, `p(b|a=1)`, and `p(b|a=0)` in a bootstrap replicate is forced to use the same repeat number.  The cached calls are not paired measurements: `rep` is only a cache-key suffix and each conditional query was independently issued.  This changes the uncertainty of the coupling difference and invalidates the pre-registered threshold calculation.  **Concrete effect:** the paper's reported 57% of 480 pair--case values above 3 bootstrap SE (`paper/main.tex:30,145`) is not reproduced with the appropriate independent per-call resampling.  I reran 5,000 bootstrap draws with independently resampled repeat indices for each `(case, condition, statement)` series: median SE changed from 0.0700 to 0.0805 nats and the fraction changed from **0.56875 (reported 57%) to 0.52708 (53%)**.  The qualitative evidence remains, but the headline percentage and the claimed three-SE criterion must be recomputed.  **Severity: major.**  **Verified by:** direct offline reconstruction of `P`, `stats`, and the bootstrap, changing only the bootstrap-index array shape from `(M,R)` to `P.shape`.

2. **`experiments/e2_hamiltonian.py:61-63` — the stored H2 `E_read` applies the wrong scale to a Score.**  The returned `score` is an expected *level index* (the cached values are about 5--6 for ten levels), not a number normalized to [0,1].  The code writes `lv0 + score * (lv[-1]-lv[0])`, multiplying by the entire 0.45-Eh span rather than the 0.05-Eh level spacing.  **Concrete effect:** the canonical `experiments/e2_raw.json` `E_read` values are unphysical positive 1.1--1.5 Eh and imply an MAE of **2.383 Eh**, not the manuscript's 104 mEh.  `experiments/figures.py:68-70` happens to remap the same raw `score` with `step=(lv[1]-lv[0])`, producing the manuscript's **0.1043 Eh (104 mEh)** MAE.  Therefore the printed number is numerically recoverable, but the data-producing script, its comment, and the stored derived field contradict it; any downstream use of `E_read` reports a materially different conclusion.  **Severity: major.**  **Verified by:** offline calculation from all 24 cached H2 responses: `mean(abs(lv0 + score*step - E_exact)) = 0.104294 Eh`; using the stored mapping gives `2.382794 Eh`.

3. **`experiments/analyze_e1e3.py:69-74` — “leave-one-out” does not leave out a molecule, only one molecule/geometry row.**  Each held-out case retains the other three stretches of the same molecule in the training fold.  Since the manuscript uses the logistic baseline to support the broad comparison in `paper/main.tex:31,186` (Jev “comparable to a two-feature logistic model”), this is an optimistic within-molecule interpolation benchmark, not a molecular generalization benchmark.  The predictors themselves are available after RHF and there is no literal target column in `X`; the problem is leakage of the correlated molecular unit through the split.  **Concrete effect:** the reported LOO AUROCs/Brier scores in `paper/main.tex:186,205-206` may be materially inflated for an unseen-molecule claim, and cannot establish the stated comparison without a grouped leave-one-molecule-out analysis (or explicitly limiting the claim to new geometries of represented molecules).  **Severity: major.**  **Verified by:** `qc_labels.py:26-30,56-60` creates 12 molecules x 4 stretches, while the fold mask at line 72 excludes only index `i`.

4. **`experiments/analyze_e1e3.py:32-41` — the reported “three bootstrap standard errors” should not be described as a calibrated false-positive criterion.**  With only three repeats, the nonparametric bootstrap distribution of the mean is a coarse resampling distribution and its standard deviation is used as a pointwise noise denominator.  It neither includes uncertainty from the finite data used to estimate the noise distribution nor controls the 480 simultaneous exceedance decisions.  This is distinct from finding 1: even after fixing the resampling independence, “57% (or 53%) exceed 3 SE” is descriptive, not evidence at a per-pair 3-sigma error rate.  **Concrete effect:** the stronger inferential wording in `paper/main.tex:145` (“our pre-registered criterion”) overstates what this calculation establishes; the raw median asymmetry and coherent-null comparison can still be reported.  **Severity: minor.**  **Verified by:** inspection of the 300-draw, three-observation bootstrap and threshold calculation; no multiplicity adjustment or calibrated reference distribution is used there.

## Verified

- **E1 sign convention / total probability (`analyze_e1e3.py:23-29`):** `J[(a,b)]` is correctly the effect of `b` on `a`, `logit p(a|b=1)-logit p(a|b=0)`, and the plotted/tested `J_ab-J_ba` compares the two directions.  The total-probability residual uses the marginal `p(b)` and the matching two conditionals.  Offline rerun reproduced 0.26 nats median asymmetry, 1.10 nats median |J|, 0.041 median absolute residual, and 0.138 90th percentile.

- **Coherent-null construction (`analyze_e1e3.py:48-62`):** it starts from an exactly compatible two-variable joint, computes both conditional log-odds directions, and applies rounding/noise before their difference.  It is a reasonable descriptive rounding/noise null, subject to the general small-repeat limitation above; it does not introduce a sign reversal.

- **E3 metrics (`analyze_e1e3.py:75-85`):** Brier is the standard mean squared probability error; fixed-width ECE bins include `p=1` in the final bin and correctly weight each nonempty bin by its sample fraction.  The offline rerun reproduced every Table 1 AUROC/Brier/ECE number to the displayed precision.  The logistic prediction for each held-out row is fitted without that row's label (the grouping caveat is finding 3, not direct target leakage).

- **Exploratory E1b/E3b values (`e1b_e3b.py:27-32`):** direct calculation from the cached raw responses reproduces the stated negation-sum range 0.89--1.25 and the two >2/3 deviations (RHF-adequate: 0.681; T1: 0.799, at the individual-repeat level).  It also reproduces geometry-only AUROCs 0.604, 0.636, 0.678 and Briers 0.311, 0.308, 0.346, which round to the manuscript values.  The code averages three repeats for E3b before scoring, as appropriate.

- **Ising conventions and rank statistic (`e2_hamiltonian.py:20-33`; `e2b_convention.py:14-19`; `figures.py:44-52`):** the original energy is `sum h_i s_i + sum J_ij s_i s_j`, and the exploratory prompt gives `-sum(-h_i)s_i - sum(-J_ij)s_i s_j`, exactly the same energy.  The rank calculation is correctly `rho(p,-E)`, and top-1 uses the minimum energy.  No sign or off-by-one defect was found.

- **H2 qubit ordering / reference (`e2_hamiltonian.py:40-50,54-59`):** OpenFermion's sparse operator uses qubit 0 as the leftmost displayed bit in this matrix representation (a `Z0` test flips basis indices 8--15).  The generator's `format(b,"04b")` is consequently consistent with the explicitly stated `q0 q1 q2 q3` order: `|1100>` occupies q0 and q1 and is the HF/dominant state.  Recomputing the cached exact weights gives `|1100>` as the largest-weight state at all eight bond lengths (0.99484 at 0.5 Å and 0.53744 at 3.0 Å), with `|0011>` second.

- **E4 determinant count, k convention, and curve averaging (`e4_selected_ci.py:23-29,67-87`):** CAS(6e,5o), (M_s=0), has `C(5,3)^2=100` determinants; `k=0..99` correctly means k non-reference determinants added to the reference, and `k=99` is the full space.  The Jev and random curves are each geometric means over 30 independently generated tie-break/permutation curves, as their caption says.  The reported Wilcoxon summaries reproduce exactly from `analyze_e4.py`: random p=0.002, excitation p=0.084, PT1 p=0.014, diagonal p=0.004.

- **E4 spin penalty (`e4_selected_ci.py:30-38,51-53`):** the subspace eigenvector is selected with `H + S^2` and its reported energy is then evaluated with unpenalized `H`, consistent with the stated convention.  A direct N2/CAS(6e,5o) reconstruction gave zero full-space penalty expectation and the same full-space H energy (-109.020300795617 Eh) from penalized and unpenalized diagonalization.  The penalty should nevertheless remain stated, because finite subspaces can have a small nonzero ⟨S^2⟩ (up to 0.00291 in the tested two-determinant N2 subspaces).

- **E4 ranking / ties (`e4_selected_ci.py:74-87,102-106`; `analyze_e4.py:7-14`):** no off-by-one bug was found.  Probability ties are broken by independent uniforms; exact-zero probabilities and distinct-value counts reproduce the manuscript's roughly 60% / 14--18 description.  The area calculation is consistently the mean log10 error across the same k grid for all methods.
