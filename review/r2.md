# Referee report R2 (emphasis: quantum-chemistry and physics correctness)

Manuscript: "Is there a Hamiltonian inside a decision model? Probing the energy landscape of Jev for quantum chemistry" (paper/main.tex, compiled main.pdf, figs/*.png).
Material examined: the manuscript, refs.bib, experiments/*.py, experiments/PLAN.md and its git history, experiments/*.json, harness/jev.py. I ran no network calls. To check the ground truth I re-ran PySCF 2.14.0 locally on the released geometries (qc_labels.json) and rebuilt the E4 Hamiltonians with the released build() function. My scripts are in my scratchpad; all numbers below that are not in the manuscript come from those re-runs or from the cached JSON, and each is labelled as such.

## Overall assessment

The paper asks a clear question: do the per-question probabilities of a multi-head typed decision model assemble into one energy landscape, and is that landscape of any use for quantum chemistry? It answers with four black-box probes against PySCF ground truth. The negative findings on in-context Hamiltonians (E2) are clean and plausible, and the convention-flip control is a good piece of design. The released cache makes every Jev-side number reproducible. I reproduced the E2 Ising summaries, the E3 table entries and the E4 Wilcoxon statistics exactly from the cached JSON.

The chemistry side is weaker than the prose suggests. On re-running the label pipeline I found that the ground truth is less settled than the manuscript implies. The RHF references are saddle points in 9 of 48 cases. The six-orbital active space splits degenerate shells in 8 cases, which makes some labels non-deterministic. The CASCI root that defines the multireference label is a triplet in 2 cases. Two T1 labels come from unconverged CCSD, and the UHF search misses at least one instability. Changing to a symmetry-complete, full-valence active space flips 3 multireference labels. Under that change the two-feature logistic baseline rises from AUROC 0.85 to 0.93 while Jev moves only from 0.74 to 0.76, so the stated parity with the descriptor model depends on the active space. In E4, Jev's determinant ranking is essentially the same for all ten molecules. Its top choice is the same HOMO to LUMO single excitation in every system, and that determinant has zero Hamiltonian coupling to the RHF reference by Brillouin's theorem. The "weak but real prior" is therefore a fixed template ordering, not system-specific information. The perturbative comparator is misnamed and handicapped by deterministic index-order tie-breaking. The H2 energy read-out uses an instrument that cannot resolve chemical accuracy, and a constant predictor beats Jev on it.

Against the five criteria:
- Originality: the black-box coupling-symmetry and total-probability protocol for a parallel typed-output model is a modest but real methodological contribution. The chemical probes themselves are standard.
- Significance: of practical interest to people considering such models for workflow triage. The broader significance is limited because the chemical test set is small, textbook and stretch-confounded (R2-M5).
- Interdisciplinary readership: accessible to ML readers. Chemists will find the ground-truth description too thin to trust without the checks requested below.
- Technical soundness: E2 Ising is sound. E3 and E4 need repairs to the ground truth and to the comparators before the comparative claims hold.
- Readability: clear, compact and well organised. Several chemical terms are used loosely ("first-order", "logically forced", "closed-shell", "exact wavefunction weight").

## Who would be interested and why

Researchers who evaluate probabilistic or LLM-style models for scientific decision support would be interested, because the paper offers a cheap black-box coherence test and a concrete negative result on in-context physics. Computational chemists who build automated multireference triage or selected-CI heuristics would read E3 and E4 for a baseline, provided the labels and comparators are made robust. Quantum-computing readers will take the E2 Jordan-Wigner result as a limit on what such models can do with qubit Hamiltonians.

## Major strengths

1. Full caching of every request and response, released with deterministic analysis scripts. I reproduced the E2 Ising summaries (for example, rho = -0.41 at n = 4 as stated, +0.34 in the flipped form), the Table 1 entries and the E4 statistics (10/10 vs random, p = 0.002; 1/10 vs PT, p = 0.014) from the cache without network access.
2. The Ising convention-flip control (E2b) is well chosen. It keeps every energy fixed and changes only how the Hamiltonian is written, which cleanly rules out "knows the energies but orders them backwards".
3. Strong, honest baselines in E3: base rate, stretch-only and stretch-plus-gap logistic models, and the geometry-only ablation that exposes the textual prior.
4. The Jordan-Wigner H2 setup is correct as far as I can verify. With OpenFermion's big-endian convention the HF state is |1100>. The ground state is correctly restricted to the N = 2 sector. The exact weights (0.995 at 0.5 Å, 0.54 at 3.0 Å) and the 204 mEh energy span match the cached exact data.
5. The NH3 (C3v, angle HNH recovered from the polar angle), CH4 (tetrahedral, bond length 1.087 f) and H2O geometry constructions in qc_labels.py are correct. The diatomic equilibrium lengths are standard experimental values.

## Major Concerns

R2-M1 [data-resource-quality]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Axis** data-resource-quality (ground-truth validity)
**Claim pointer** Setup and E3 state that the three binary labels (multireference from the largest CASCI(6o) coefficient squared below 0.9, T1 > 0.02 from CCSD, RHF unstable if UHF is more than 0.1 mEh lower) are reliable physical ground truth, and that Jev's AUROC of 0.74 to 0.89 is "comparable to a two-feature logistic model".
**Evidence pointer** Section 3 "Chemistry ground truth"; Table 1; experiments/qc_labels.py; experiments/qc_labels.json.
**Concern** Re-running the released pipeline on the released geometries exposes several defects that the manuscript does not report.
(a) RHF saddle points. qc_labels.py never checks RHF internal stability or `mf.converged`. An RHF internal stability analysis finds a lower RHF solution in 9 of 48 cases: N2 x1.5/2.0/2.5 (30, 191 and 289 mEh lower), CO x2.5 (256), C2 x1.0/2.0/2.5 (30, 59 and 158), H2O x2.5 (26) and NH3 x2.5 (21). CASCI orbitals, the CCSD reference, the HOMO-LUMO gap used by the logistic baseline, and the "UHF minus RHF" drop are all computed from these saddle points. For C2 at equilibrium, part of the 118 mEh label drop is RHF-to-RHF relaxation, not RHF-to-UHF instability.
(b) Active space that breaks symmetry. The rule "six orbitals, min(6, N) electrons", with orbitals taken by energy, splits a degenerate shell at the upper boundary for all four CH4 cases, H2 x2.0/2.5, HF x1.0/1.5 and F2 x2.0. The resulting c0^2 depends on an arbitrary rotation within the degenerate pair or triplet. It is not reproducible. My re-run gives CH4 x1.5 c0^2 = 0.9395 and CH4 x2.0 = 0.5772, against the stored 0.9408 and 0.5676. The same rule includes the Li 1s and B 1s cores in the active space for LiH and BH but cuts the valence space for N2, CO, F2, NH3 and CH4, so the "multireference" measure is not the same quantity across molecules.
(c) Wrong spin state. `mcscf.CASCI` is run without a spin constraint, and for C2 x1.5 and CH4 x2.5 the lowest root is a triplet (<S^2> = 2.000). In both cases the HF determinant has zero weight in the labelled state. The prompt shown to Jev, however, defines the statement as "the Hartree-Fock determinant carries less than 90% of the exact wavefunction weight". The label (largest coefficient of a truncated CASCI, sometimes of a triplet root) is not the quantity the prompt describes.
(d) Active-space sensitivity. With a symmetry-complete full-valence space and a singlet constraint, 3 labels flip (BeO x2.0: 0.934 to 0.794; BeO x2.5: 0.947 to 0.846; CH4 x1.5: 0.94 to 0.893). With CAS(8,8), H2O x1.5 also flips. On the cached Jev probabilities, full-valence labels give Jev AUROC 0.76 against 0.93 for the stretch-plus-gap logistic model, whereas the paper reports 0.74 against 0.85. The non-monotonic BeO c0^2 in the paper's own labels (0.853, 0.934, 0.947 at x1.5/2.0/2.5) is itself a warning sign.
(e) Unconverged CCSD. `ccsd_converged` is False for N2 x2.5 and CO x2.5, yet their T1 values are used as labels. With all-electron versus frozen-core normalisation, 3 T1 labels flip (F2 x1.5, F2 x2.5, BH x1.5). The manuscript does not say which convention was used, and the Lee and Taylor threshold is conventionally applied to valence-correlated amplitudes.
(f) Incomplete UHF search. The search perturbs one diagonal element of the density on AO 0 and applies one stability step. A HOMO-LUMO mixing guess with iterated stability following finds a UHF solution 0.185 mEh below RHF for CH4 x1.5, so that label flips from stable to unstable under the paper's own 0.1 mEh threshold. The same search shows that the stored drops are often far from the lowest UHF solution (BH x2.0: 0.59 against 41.5 mEh; CH4 x2.0: 96 against 213 mEh; BeO x1.5: 64 against 110 mEh). A 0.1 mEh threshold also sits close to SCF convergence noise.
**Why it matters** E3's comparative conclusion (Jev comparable to a descriptor model) and the E1 ground-truth context depend on these labels. Single-label flips move AUROC noticeably with n = 48 and only 11 negatives for "RHF unstable". The active-space choice alone moves the descriptor baseline by 0.07 AUROC. As it stands, a chemist cannot tell which differences in Table 1 come from Jev and which come from the labelling protocol.
**Resolution test** Regenerate the labels with (i) RHF internal stability following to a true RHF minimum plus a `converged` check; (ii) a symmetry-complete active space (full valence, or AVAS or an equivalent automated selection, which the paper already cites) with a singlet constraint (`fix_spin_(ss=0)`), and the HF-determinant weight reported alongside the largest weight; (iii) converged CCSD for every case, or exclusion of unconverged cases, with the frozen-core convention stated; (iv) a multi-start UHF search with iterated stability following, and a threshold well above SCF tolerance (for example 1 mEh), with a sensitivity table at 0.1, 1 and 10 mEh. Then report Table 1 under the primary and at least one alternative label definition, with bootstrap intervals. The "comparable to a two-feature logistic model" claim should stand only if it survives these changes.

R2-M2 [mechanism-evidence]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** mechanism-evidence
**Claim pointer** E4 is titled "a weak but real prior for selected configuration interaction". The abstract states that as an importance prior Jev beats random selection in all ten systems, implying that Jev extracts system-specific information about which determinants matter.
**Evidence pointer** Section 4.4 (E4); Fig. 4b,c; experiments/e4_raw.json (jev_probs).
**Concern** Every E4 system uses the same CAS(6e,5o) label set, so Jev's probability vectors can be compared directly across molecules. From the cached responses, the median cross-system Spearman correlation between Jev's determinant probability vectors is 0.94 (minimum 0.87; median Pearson 0.98). The top five determinants are identical in order for H2O x1.0 and F2 x1.5 (22ab0, 20220, 22002, 22020, 220ab). Jev's single most probable determinant is "22ab0" in all ten systems. That is the HOMO to LUMO single excitation, which has zero coupling to the canonical RHF reference by Brillouin's theorem. It is never the oracle's top determinant (0/10; in every system the oracle's top determinant is a double excitation). Jev's positive Spearman correlation with |c_i|^2 (0.15 to 0.49) is therefore what a fixed "low excitations near the Fermi level first" template would earn, and that is essentially the excitation-rank heuristic, which the paper finds statistically indistinguishable from Jev (7/10, p = 0.08).
**Why it matters** The E4 interpretation, and the abstract's framing of Jev as an importance prior, credit Jev with reading the molecule. The data show a molecule-independent ordering over occupation strings. That is a different and more important negative finding, and it also puts the "beats random" result in context.
**Resolution test** Add a leave-one-system-out control that ranks each system with Jev's probabilities pooled from the other nine systems, and show whether per-system Jev beats it. Report the cross-system similarity of Jev's rankings. State in the abstract and E4 text that Jev does not beat the excitation-rank heuristic, which uses the same inputs Jev sees. Note that Jev's preferred determinant is a Brillouin-null single excitation.

R2-M3 [experimental-design]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** experimental-design
**Claim pointer** E4 states that Jev "is worse than the first-order estimate in 9 of 10", that the estimate |<D_i|H|D_0>|^2/(H_ii - H_00) is "first-order", and that energies come from diagonalising "with a spin penalty that keeps the lowest root a singlet".
**Evidence pointer** Section 4.4; Fig. 4b; experiments/e4_selected_ci.py (curves, build).
**Concern** (a) Naming. |H_i0|^2/(H_ii - H_00) is the Epstein-Nesbet second-order energy contribution, which is the CIPSI selection criterion. The first-order amplitude squared would be |H_i0|^2/(H_ii - H_00)^2. The two orderings differ. (b) Tie handling. The estimate is non-zero only for determinants directly coupled to the reference (16 to 38 of 99 in my rebuild; singles vanish by Brillouin). The remaining 60 to 80 determinants are then appended in Python's stable-sort index order, which is visible as the long plateau of the PT curve in Fig. 4b between k of about 20 and 78. Jev's zero-probability ties are instead broken randomly and averaged over 30 draws with a geometric mean. The two comparators are therefore not tie-broken the same way, and the area metric over k = 0 to 99 mostly measures the tie region. (c) Spin penalty. H + 1.0 S^2 is applied to spin-incomplete determinant subspaces, and <H> of the penalised lowest vector is reported. This is not the standard selected-CI variational energy. The penalty weight (1 Eh) is not stated in the manuscript, and the curves may depend on it. (d) Active-space symmetry. CAS(6e,5o) splits a degenerate shell at the upper boundary in 4 of the 10 E4 systems (F2 x1.5, C2 x1.0, NH3 x2.0, CH4 x2.0), so the oracle weights and the "exact" target depend on an arbitrary orbital rotation. C2 x1.0 is also built on an internally unstable RHF (item a of R2-M1). (e) Question and use do not match. Jev is asked for the single largest-weight determinant and the answer is then used as a full ranking. The about 60% exact zeros are at least partly a consequence of eliciting a top-1 answer, not only of "resolution". (f) Same-geometry c0^2 values differ between E3 and E4 (CH4 x2.0: 0.568 in the CAS(6,6) label, 0.728 in CAS(6,5); CO x1.5: 0.742 against 0.857), which the reader is not told.
**Why it matters** The E4 ordering of methods (oracle, PT, Jev, excitation, diagonal, random) is the quantitative basis for "dominated by a free perturbative estimate and should not be used". The conclusion is probably right in direction, but the size of each gap depends on tie-breaking, the penalty construction and the active-space choice.
**Resolution test** Rename the comparator (Epstein-Nesbet second-order contribution, or CIPSI criterion), and optionally add the true first-order |c^(1)|^2 ordering. Break ties randomly for every method, or rank the uncoupled remainder by a second iteration (iterative CIPSI) as a realistic selected-CI baseline. Use spin-complete subspaces (add all spin partners of each open-shell determinant, or work with CSFs) instead of a penalty, or report the penalty weight and show that results do not change for, say, lambda = 0.5, 1 and 5 Eh. Use symmetry-complete active spaces. Report a metric aligned with the question asked (top-1 match rate, or the rank of the oracle top determinant), or re-ask Jev for a ranking.

R2-M4 [experimental-design]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** experimental-design (measurement instrument)
**Claim pointer** The abstract states that Jev's energy read-out for H2 "is off by 104 mEh on average", and the Discussion states that its energies "are two orders of magnitude from chemical accuracy".
**Evidence pointer** Section 4.2, last paragraph; Fig. 3c; experiments/e2_hamiltonian.py (h2_ask); e2_raw.json.
**Concern** The read-out is the expectation over a ten-level Score spanning -1.20 to -0.75 Eh in 50 mEh steps. Snapping the exact energies to the nearest level already gives an MAE of about 7 mEh (my computation from the cached exact energies), so the instrument cannot express chemical accuracy whatever the model knows. Comparing to 1.6 mEh is therefore a statement about the instrument. The informative comparison is with an uninformed predictor. A constant equal to the median exact energy has MAE 65 mEh, and the scale midpoint (-0.975 Eh) has 71 mEh. Jev's 104 mEh is worse than both, because its Score distributions are nearly flat with about 25% on the highest level (-0.75 Eh) at every bond length. Jev also puts 12 to 23% (mean 17%) of the "dominant basis state" probability on states outside the stated two-particle sector, although the prompt gives particle_number = 2. That is direct evidence that it does not use the sector constraint, and the paper does not report it. Choosing |1100> needs no Hamiltonian reading, since it is the textbook Jordan-Wigner HF state for H2. The dominant-state test therefore cannot separate reading from recall. The paper partly acknowledges this by noting that the probability stays near 0.2.
**Why it matters** The headline 104 mEh figure and the "two orders of magnitude" sentence overstate what the experiment measures. The more telling results (worse than a constant; probability mass outside the stated particle sector) are missing.
**Resolution test** Report the constant-predictor and level-snapping baselines next to the 104 mEh figure. Drop or qualify the chemical-accuracy comparison. Report the probability mass on states that violate particle number and S_z. Optionally, relabel the qubits or permute the spin-orbital order so that the HF state is not |1100>, which tests whether the dominant-state answer comes from the printed Hamiltonian or from recall.

R2-M5 [claim-moderation]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** claim-moderation
**Claim pointer** The Discussion says Jev "could route molecules between single- and multireference methods before any calculation" and ranks multireference character and RHF instability "about as well as a small descriptor model".
**Evidence pointer** Section 4.3; Table 1; Discussion paragraph 3; qc_labels.json.
**Concern** The case set is 12 small main-group molecules, each stretched symmetrically by a factor that is stated in the prompt. The labels largely encode that factor: "RHF unstable" agrees with "stretch > 1" in 43 of 48 cases, and the multireference and T1 labels in 38 of 48 (my tally from qc_labels.json). The stretch-only logistic model reaches AUROC 0.87 on RHF instability. E3 therefore mostly measures whether Jev reads "all bonds stretched to 2.0 x equilibrium", which the geometry-only ablation supports. Realistic multireference triage concerns equilibrium structures of transition-metal complexes, diradicals and similar systems, which is the setting of the works cited for multireference diagnostics (Jiang et al. 2012; Duan et al. 2020). The test set contains none of them. With n = 48, AUROC differences such as 0.74 against 0.85 are also reported without intervals, although the pre-registered plan promised bootstrap 95% CIs. Leave-one-out refitting is done by case, so other stretches of the same molecule stay in the training fold.
**Why it matters** The practical recommendation (triage with recalibration) is the paper's main positive message to chemists, and the present evidence does not support it beyond stretched textbook molecules.
**Resolution test** Restrict the triage claim to the tested regime, or add an equilibrium-geometry set with genuine variation in multireference character (for example diradicals, transition-metal diatomics or ozone-like systems) with no stretch note. Report bootstrap CIs for AUROC, Brier and ECE, and a paired test of Jev against the logistic model. Use leave-one-molecule-out fitting for the descriptor baselines.

R2-M6 [reproducibility]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Axis** reproducibility (pre-registration fidelity)
**Claim pointer** Setup says the E1 to E4 analyses were pre-registered before the main data and that checks added afterwards are labelled exploratory.
**Evidence pointer** Section 3 "Pre-registration"; experiments/PLAN.md at commit 8ee0321 and HEAD; experiments/e1e3_collect.py.
**Concern** The pre-registered E1 statement set was "multireference; RHF qualitatively correct; open-shell ground state; CCSD(T) within 1 kcal/mol; low-lying excited state within 1 eV". The collected and reported set replaces the open-shell and excited-state statements with "T1 > 0.02" and "RHF unstable". Neither PLAN.md's amendment section nor the manuscript discloses this. The pre-registered positive control for E1 (an exact Ising model's conditionals and number statements) does not appear, and the post hoc simulated null is presented as its replacement without being labelled a deviation. The pre-registered maximum-likelihood inverse temperature beta for E2 is not reported. The promised bootstrap 95% CIs are absent from Table 1 and the E4 comparisons. The E4 summary metric (area under the log-error curve) and the paired Wilcoxon tests are not in the plan, and the E4 spin penalty and excitation baseline, which PLAN.md lists as post-data changes, are not marked exploratory in the manuscript. The plan targeted about 60 cases; 48 were used.
**Why it matters** The paper uses pre-registration as a credibility claim. Undisclosed changes to the statement set and to primary metrics weaken that claim, even if each change is reasonable.
**Resolution test** Add a short deviations table (planned, done, reason) to the paper or a supplement. Mark the E1 statement change, the null-model substitution, the E4 metric and baselines, and the case count as deviations. Report beta or explain why it was dropped.

## Minor Comments

R2-m1 [writing-clarity]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Description of the multireference coupling in E1 as "logically forced".
**Evidence pointer** Section 4.1, paragraph 2; e1e3_collect.py statement texts.
**Issue** "The ground state has strong multireference character" and "RHF is a qualitatively correct reference" are closely related chemically but not logical negations, and the prompt's multireference criterion (HF weight below 90%) does not imply RHF inadequacy.

R2-m2 [writing-clarity]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Discussion sentence stating that a Gibbs sampler on Jev's conditionals converges to an update-order-dependent distribution.
**Evidence pointer** Discussion, paragraph 1.
**Issue** A Gibbs sampler needs full conditionals p(a | all other statements). E1 measured pairwise conditionals with the other statements marginalised, and no sampler was run. The sentence states a general property of incompatible conditionals as if it had been shown for Jev.
**Required correction** Phrase it as a consequence that would follow if full conditionals were similarly incompatible, or run the sampler on measured full conditionals.

## Technical failings before the case is established

- R2-M1: the E3 ground-truth labels need regenerating with stable RHF references, symmetry-complete singlet active spaces, converged CCSD with a stated T1 convention, and a robust UHF search, and Table 1 needs recomputing with intervals and label-sensitivity variants before the comparative E3 claim can stand.

## Recommendation posture

The E2 in-context Hamiltonian result is sound and useful. The E1 protocol is a reasonable contribution. The quantum-chemistry half (E3 and E4) is currently not established from the provided evidence, because the ground truth has correctable but material defects and the E4 "prior" is a fixed template ordering. I would be supportive if the technical concerns are resolved. Most fixes are inexpensive, since the Jev responses are cached and only the PySCF side and the analyses need re-running, but the E3 and E4 claims should be restated after those re-runs.
