# Gaps this paper addresses (novelty statement)

Keys refer to `refs.bib`. Each gap is a claim that the survey found *no prior test*. It rests on the works cited, not on an exhaustive search. Before submission, re-check each gap against papers posted after 2026-09-23.

1. **No test of whether a parallel, native-distribution decision model has a joint.** Compatibility of conditionals has been worked out since the 1970s [besag1974spatial, hammersley1971markov, arnold1989compatible], and dependency networks show that separately learned conditionals are generally inconsistent [heckerman2000dependency]. LLM coherence work tests identities on generative, sampled or verbalised probabilities [fluri2023evaluating, paleka2025consistency, zhu2024incoherent]. No work applies an Arnold–Press ratio test or a Gibbs-joint test to a model that returns many question-conditional distributions in parallel over one shared state, which is Jev's design [typesafe2026api, willison2026jev].

2. **No measurement of coupling symmetry J_ij = J_ji or of cycle consistency in a model's conditionals.** Inverse-Ising and Hamiltonian-learning methods assume the data come from a real Gibbs state [nguyen2017inverse, anshu2021sample, wiebe2014hamiltonian]. We instead fit single-site log-odds responses and measure the antisymmetric part of the inferred coupling matrix, together with path dependence around closed loops. Both are direct tests of whether E = −log p is one landscape or several. The vendor concedes that invariants are not guaranteed and reports a negation pair summing to 1.19 [typesafe2026jaggedness], but it does not quantify the incoherence.

3. **Calibration versus coherence has not been separated for a model trained for calibration.** Calibration work scores marginals against outcomes [guo2017calibration, naeini2015obtaining, kadavath2022language, tian2023just], and proper-scoring theory [gneiting2007strictly, brier1950verification] rewards each head independently. No study asks whether a model trained explicitly for calibrated decisions (RLCD, [typesafe2026introducing]) can be calibrated per question and still be jointly incoherent. The dependency-network argument [heckerman2000dependency] says it can.

4. **No test of whether a language-conditioned model can rank the spectrum of a physical qubit Hamiltonian.** Quantum-chemistry encodings (Jordan–Wigner H2, Ising/QUBO) are used to *compute* ground states on quantum, annealing or neural hardware [jordan1928pauli, omalley2016scalable, xia2018electronic, genin2019quantum, carleo2017solving, pfau2020ab, hermann2020deep, lucas2014ising]. None tests whether a general decision model given the Hamiltonian as text orders basis states or eigenstates by energy. The vendor's stated numeric weaknesses [typesafe2026jaggedness] and the known numeric fragility of LLMs [mirzadeh2024gsm] make this a real test and not a formality.

5. **No evaluation of a general calibrated decision model on quantum-chemistry triage labels.** Multireference diagnosis, spin-state ordering, active-space choice and determinant importance have so far been learned by task-specific models trained on computed descriptors [duan2020data, liu2020rapid, janet2017predicting, jeong2020automation, coe2018machine], or handled by physics heuristics [lee1989diagnostic, jiang2012multireference, sayfutyarova2017automated, holmes2016heat, huron1973iterative]. None scores a zero-shot, text-conditioned calibrated model against these labels with proper scoring rules.

6. **No check of whether a scientific decision layer's confidences follow physics or surface features.** ChemBench reports overconfident LLM predictions in chemistry [mirza2025framework], and agent systems [bran2024augmenting, boiko2023autonomous] call LLMs without calibrated gating. No prior work asks whether a decision model's confidence on multireference or spin-state questions tracks the physical quantity (T1, D1, ΔE_spin) or the surface form of the molecule's name or formula. Controlled paraphrase and relabelling can separate the two.

7. **Classifier-as-EBM and network-as-Hamiltonian analyses have not reached multi-query structured-output models.** Existing work reads one softmax head as an energy [grathwohl2019your] or treats a random MLP as a Hamiltonian over its inputs [winer2025deep]. It relates attention to Hopfield energies [ramsauer2020hopfield] but does not ask whether separate, typed output heads of a deployed proprietary model share one energy function. We ask that question from black-box API access alone.
